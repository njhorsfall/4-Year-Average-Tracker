How the signals work

4-year average = the mean of every daily closing price over the last four years (split-adjusted, not dividend-adjusted). It's treated as the stock's fair price.
▼ BUY when the latest price is at or below the 4-year average and earnings are healthy.
◆ WATCH when it's at or below the average but earnings fail a check or have no data. It might be a bargain, or a value trap.
▲ SELL when the latest price is at least the chosen threshold above the average.
HOLD anywhere in between. Stocks with less than of 4 years' history show N/A, because their average isn't comparable yet.

Healthy earnings means passing every check that has data: profitable in the latest year; earnings per share (EPS) at least as high as three years ago; no more than a 10% drop on the previous year; and analysts' next-year estimate at or above the last 12 months, if there is one. The first two must have data.

Prices: Yahoo Finance via yfinance, end-of-day. FTSE prices are in pence (p). This is a screening tool, not financial advice.
